Detailed business plan

The operating thesis.

Concept & positioning

Transform high-quality 3–5 bedroom residential homes into professionally operated, low-density residences. The product sits between informal flat-sharing, conventional co-living and serviced apartments. The resident buys a home experience plus an operating layer, not merely a furnished bed.

Target customer

Working professionals roughly 22–40: relocating employees, startup teams, founders, consultants, project staff and people between leases. Multi-month stays form the economic core. Women-only homes become a dedicated vertical once demand is validated.

Product architecture

Spacious 3–5BHK homes in desirable micro-markets, normally 4–6 residents per 4BHK. Private and twin rooms. Furnishing, Wi-Fi, housekeeping, linen, transparent utilities, maintenance, digital access and optional partner-led food, cook, laundry, gym and mobility services.

Operations

Build dense neighbourhood clusters. Share housekeeping, maintenance, laundry, food partners and community operations across nearby properties. Screen and internally match residents using tenure, work location, WFH pattern, smoking, cleanliness, guest preferences and other practical compatibility factors.

Revenue model

Primary accommodation fees from private and twin rooms; selective short-stay yield on vacant compliant inventory; service margins/commissions; corporate room blocks; and, over time, management/performance fees from owner inventory.

Go-to-market

Resident demand: property-led digital acquisition, Google intent, referrals, startup/coworking communities, corporate HR and relocation, women-focused professional communities and selective brokers. Owner supply: brokers, direct-owner outreach, landlord referrals and a clear yield + asset-care proposition.

Key risks

Regulation: obtain formal legal/tax advice on tenancy, accommodation/PG classification, safety and GST before launch. Rent: strict acquisition ceiling. Vacancy: cluster demand and corporate channels. Resident conflict: screening, rules and transfer capability. Service complexity: keep ancillary services partner-led initially.

Roadmap

Phase 1: three-home pilot. Phase 2: 10–20 home dense cluster. Phase 3: multiple Bangalore micro-markets. Phase 4: shift more supply to management agreements and owner-funded inventory. Phase 5: replicate the operating system in additional cities.

The long-term asset is the network: owner relationships, resident demand, operating systems, cluster density, matching intelligence and trusted brand.
